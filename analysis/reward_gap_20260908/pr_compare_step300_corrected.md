**Reading the curves (per-100-step means of the tfevents series):**
- **Reward.** BF16's mean rollout reward climbs 0.122 -> 0.185 -> 0.252 over steps 1-100 / 101-200 / 201-300; NVFP4's is flat at 0.077 -> 0.094 -> 0.092. BF16 is ahead from about step 10 on (steps 1-10 are within noise; steps 11-30 already +0.04-0.05, ~2 SE), but both are roughly flat for the first ~100 steps; BF16 starts climbing around step 100-120 while NVFP4 does not, and the gap widens steadily (+0.04 over steps 1-50 to +0.18 over 251-300). Per-step reward over 64 samples is noisy (sigma 0.05-0.08), so the trailing-10 curves only stay >2 SE apart from ~step 140, while the per-100-step means separate at 4.6 / 7.7 / 11.8 SE; over 201-300 BF16 is 2.7x NVFP4.
- **Learning signal.** The zero-std group fraction (all 8 samples of a prompt got the same reward, so the group carries no advantage) falls for BF16 (0.63 -> 0.53 -> 0.49) and stays high for NVFP4 (0.72 -> 0.71 -> 0.70). Zero-std groups are kept in the batch in this recipe (`drop_zero_std_reward_groups=False`; 8 groups / 64 samples every step in both runs) but carry zero advantage, so over steps 201-300 only ~2.4 of NVFP4's 8 groups per step (30%) contribute gradient vs ~4.1 of 8 (51%) for BF16, while the loss is normalized over all 64 samples' tokens either way - the NVFP4 update is diluted. NVFP4's lower grad norm (0.12 vs 0.17) and loss are consistent with that.
- **Trainer-generator mismatch.** NVFP4's per-token logprob difference is ~4.3x BF16's in the mean (-0.0029 vs -0.0007) and ~3.5x in the per-step max (3.9 vs 1.1 over 201-300; peaks 36.0 at step 249 vs 14.7 at step 195), and its PPO clip fraction is ~6.4x (0.021 vs 0.003). Entropy (0.28 both) and response length (~1400-1440 tokens both; 1437 vs 1421 over 201-300) track each other step for step (r ~ 0.96) - expected, since both runs see the same prompts at each step.
- **Throughput.** NVFP4 steps take 1.54x longer over steps 1-300 (613 vs 399 s) and 1.66x over 201-300 (644 vs 388 s). Trainer fwd+bwd is 37 vs 22 s of that, so the gap is in rollout generation: the generator's vLLM engine step (= inter-token latency at these batch sizes) is 263 vs 171 ms median (1.53x) at equal response length, i.e. NVFP4 decode is slower per token. In this build the 4over6 rollout path re-quantizes the BF16 expert weights on every forward call (no inference weight cache); an earlier same-node microbenchmark (`ablation/bench_4over6_requant.py`, not part of these runs) attributed roughly 60% of the MoE-kernel gap to that re-quantization. Weight pulls are identical (3.1 s per rank per sync) - the NVLink sync path is not the difference. Wall-clock to step 300 is 66.1 h vs 35.2 h; NVFP4's includes the window-3 hang (3 h 20 min in its first weight pull, 09-05; `ablation/CHAINED_RUNS.md`, Fault handling) and the window-4 loss (steps 70-100 recomputed, ~8 h; lineage note above). BF16 had no outage before step 300.
